Catchy slogans and deep-dive essays addressing pressing local issues like climate change, mining, and water pollution.

John Benedict P. Supnet

🌍 Topic: Mining

"Mining poison sa among suba, patay na ang Davao forever — Stop it now!"

This slogan carries a clear and urgent warning. Literally, it means that waste and toxic materials from mining activities are being dumped into Davao’s rivers, slowly destroying them. The phrase “mining poison” refers to the heavy silt, sediments, and harmful chemicals that flow from mining sites into waterways. “Patay na ang Davao forever” emphasizes that if this continues, the damage to our environment will become permanent, affecting the health of rivers that many communities depend on for water, fishing, and agriculture. On a deeper level, the slogan symbolizes the long-term consequences of prioritizing short-term economic gains over environmental protection. Rivers are the lifeblood of Davao. When they are poisoned by mining waste, it does not only kill aquatic life but also threatens the livelihoods of farmers and fisherfolk, increases flooding risks, and endangers public health. The message is a strong call to action — we must stop the destruction before it reaches a point of no return. It reminds us that environmental damage today will rob future generations of a healthy and livable Davao. I chose the topic of mining impacts on rivers because it is highly relevant to our region. In areas like Davao Oriental and parts of Davao City, nickel mining and quarrying have caused serious siltation and pollution in rivers such as the Mapagba, Pintatagan, and sections of the Davao River. These activities have turned clear rivers into muddy, rust-colored waters, especially after heavy rains. As a resident of Davao, I believe we have a responsibility to protect our natural resources. Stronger enforcement of environmental laws, responsible mining practices, and community awareness are needed. It is time for all sectors — government, companies, and citizens — to work together to stop the poison and preserve Davao’s rivers for the future.

🌍 Topic: Pollution

“Tamang Paglabay Sa Basura, Permi Sa Hunahuna—Aron Naa Pay Ugma.”

This slogan reminds me, and everyone else, to always keep proper waste disposal in mind. Literally, it means that if I make it a habit to throw garbage in the right place every day, I help protect the future. The phrase “permi sa hunahuna” pushes me to stay consistent, not just when it’s convenient but all the time. Meanwhile, “naa pay ugma” reminds me that my small actions today can affect what tomorrow will look like. It is simple, but it reflects something I should practice daily, not just say. On a deeper level, I see this slogan as a reminder of personal responsibility. I know that sometimes I can be careless or think that one small piece of trash will not matter, but when I really think about it, those small actions add up. If I ignore proper waste disposal, I become part of the problem. But if I stay mindful, I become part of the solution. “Permi sa hunahuna” feels like a challenge to myself to build better habits, while “naa pay ugma” represents the kind of environment I want to help protect. It reminds me that caring for the environment is not just a big idea, but something I practice through everyday choices. I chose this topic because I have personally seen how improper garbage disposal affects our surroundings in Davao. There are times when I notice trash on the streets or in canals, especially after it rains, and I realize that I am also part of the community responsible for keeping it clean. This made me reflect on my own actions and how I can do better. By using Bisaya in my slogan, I feel like I am speaking not just to others, but also to myself in a more personal way. I want to be more mindful and consistent, and hopefully encourage others to do the same. If I start with myself and continue doing the right thing, even in small ways, I believe it can help create a cleaner environment and a better future for all of us.

Christian Jhon Ed J. Rosal

🌍 Topic: Water Pollution

"Dirty creeks today, a lifeless gulf tomorrow—clear the flow!"

Dirty creeks today, a lifeless gulf tomorrow—clear the flow! Walk through Agdao on a gloomy rainy afternoon, and just by glancing at it, you know what the slogan means without anyone explaining it. The creeks overflow, plastic sachets and food wrappers swirl into drainage channels that, if not clogged, are still full of garbage. Water pumps choke until the streets are knee-deep in black floodwater. That is the literal picture and imagery shown in the slogan — urban waterways that have become open dumpsites with just a twist of water, so clogged with uncollected solid waste and domestic garbage that they cannot do their one sole job: to deliver water out to the open seas. "Clear the flow" is not a poetic phrase, but a directly imperative one — an instruction. To remove the blockages, stop dumping in the esteros, and actually show up when initiatives are laid out for clean-up drives. The slogan was written to live on a poster — short enough to shout and chant, and honest enough to deliver a message. But the deeper message is about what happens after the floodwater recedes and the streets dry out from the rain. The plastic that chokes the Davao River and Agdao Creek does not disappear — it eventually finds its way into the Davao Gulf, one of the most biologically diverse and rich stretches of ocean in the Philippines. Marine biologists here have performed studies and investigations, particularly on the whales living in the area. Washed ashore and dead, their stomachs full not of fish but of human waste — food wrappers, plastic sachets, nylon ropes, and plastic cellophanes. The animals showed no signs of illness or poor health. They simply starved because garbage filled them up with no room left for actual food. "A lifeless gulf tomorrow" is not an exaggeration. The slogan encapsulates what ecologists call the ridge-to-reef continuum: what happens in the streets and waterways of the city does not stay there — it flushes out to the nearest body of water, and becomes a permanent, lethal presence in the marine food web. From Davao City to the entirety of Davao del Sur, this is not an invisible dilemma. Landfills have been over capacity since 2023, and a city generating roughly 450 tons of garbage every single day has nowhere for it to flow but to the seas. This topic was not chosen just for the sake of submitting an output. It was chosen because I am here — in Agdao — suffering daily from floods that seem to have no source. Even when it is not raining, it floods, and it is miserable. From the whale shark struggling in the gulf just a few kilometers away, to me struggling to get home because of the danger of leptospirosis — we are both struggling.

🌍 Topic: Climate Change

“Tick Tock Tick Tock, Climate Change is Around the Clock”

“Tick Tock Tick Tock, Climate Change is Around the Clock” is a slogan about climate change that highlights the urgency of the Earth’s ever rising temperatures due to global warming. Literally speaking, the “tick tock tick tock” represents the sound of a clock, constantly reminding us that time is continuously moving forward. And with “Climate Change is around the clock”, this issue is something that happens all the time, 24/7. With these in mind, the slogan reminds us that climate change is not just a one-time issue, but rather a danger that grows larger every second of every day. The slogan carries a deeper message about urgency, and responsibility. The ticking clock symbolizes a countdown of a doomsday clock, showing that humanity only has a limited amount of time to act before the consequences become irreversible. This also reflects how climate change is a constant, invisible force that affects wildlife, ecosystems, weather patterns, as well as human lives even when we are not paying attention. The imagery of the Earth’s countdown invokes a sense of pressure and unease, encouraging people to recognize that inaction only accelerates the problem and that contributing to nature NOW is essential. I chose this topic because climate change is not just a global issue—it is something that directly affects communities like ours here in Davao City. In places like the Philippines, typhoons, rising temperatures, and flooding are becoming more common, impacting livelihoods and safety. In my experience as well, I am also a victim of high-level floods, reaching up to the 2nd Building of my Dormitory. As a student who is in fact aware of the dangers of flood, this makes the message of urgency even more personal and relevant. By using this slogan, I am hoping that it raises awareness and inspires others to take small but meaningful actions in the present, such as conserving energy, reducing waste, and supporting environmental initiatives. If we recognize that climate change is happening “around the clock,” then our efforts to fight it must be just as constant and committed.

🌍 Topic: Water Pollution

"Pollute the stream, destroy the dream—clean water keeps life supreme."

This slogan directly points to the visible problem of water pollution and its immediate consequences. Literally, it means that when rivers, streams, and other bodies of water are contaminated, the quality of life for everyone is affected. Water is not only for drinking; it is essential for cooking, farming, bathing, and sustaining ecosystems. Once polluted, water becomes a carrier of disease instead of life. The slogan highlights the contrast between destruction and preservation, reminding us that clean water is a basic requirement for a healthy community. Beyond its literal meaning, the slogan carries a deeper message about responsibility and foresight. “Destroy the dream” symbolizes how pollution affects future generations who depend on these water sources. It represents lost opportunities for sustainable living, food security, and environmental balance. The phrase “life supreme” emphasizes that water is at the top of our survival needs, more important than many things we take for granted. The rhyme is meant to make the message memorable, but the core idea is serious: neglecting our waterways is equivalent to neglecting our future. Clean water reflects discipline, respect for nature, and collective accountability. I chose water pollution because it is a visible and relatable issue in Davao. Many communities rely on rivers and coastal areas for their livelihood, yet improper waste disposal, plastic pollution, and drainage runoff continue to threaten these waters. Seeing trash in canals after heavy rains and hearing about polluted rivers made me realize how ordinary habits contribute to a larger problem. This topic feels personal because clean water should be something we never worry about. Through this slogan and essay, I want to encourage small but meaningful actions—proper waste disposal, reducing plastic use, and being more mindful of how our actions affect the water that sustains us.
